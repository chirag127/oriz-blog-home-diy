You do not need to renovate to make a room feel new. Most of what reads as
"tired" is fixable in a weekend for the cost of a couple of takeaways — because
what you notice in a room is rarely the furniture. It is the light, the contrast,
and the clutter. Fix those three and the room resets.

Budget: modest, itemized below. Time: one weekend. Skill: beginner. Renting? Every
step here is reversible.

## Start by taking everything off the surfaces

Before spending anything, clear every flat surface — shelves, sills, the coffee
table — into a box. A room reads as calm when surfaces breathe. You will put back
about half, and the room already looks better. This step is free and does more
than most purchases.

## Move one: fix the light

Bad lighting makes a good room feel flat, and one harsh ceiling bulb is the usual
offender. Two cheap changes:

- **Swap the bulbs to a warm tone** (around 2700K, sold as "warm white"). Cool
  bulbs read like an office. Warm light reads like home. A few dollars per bulb.
- **Add one lamp at a lower level** — a table or floor lamp in a corner. A single
  low, warm light source does more for how a room feels in the evening than any
  amount of ceiling light.

Aim for light at three heights: ceiling, table, and floor. You rarely need all
three on at once, but having the option changes the room.

## Move two: paint one wall

You do not need to repaint the whole room. One wall — the one you face when you
enter — in a considered color resets the whole space.

1. Wipe the wall, fill any small holes, let dry.
2. Tape the edges along the ceiling, skirting, and adjacent walls.
3. Cut in the edges with a brush, then roll the middle in a W pattern for even
   coverage. Two coats.
4. Pull the tape while the second coat is still slightly wet for a crisp line.

Cost: a small tin covers one wall twice. Renting? Choose a shade close enough to
the existing walls that you can paint back easily, or ask your landlord — many
say yes to neutral colors.

## Move three: layer texture

Flat rooms are usually low on texture, not low on stuff. Add contrast in
material, not more objects:

- A throw folded over a chair or sofa arm.
- Two cushions in a fabric different from the sofa — linen against smooth, or a
  weave against flat.
- One rug to anchor the seating. A rug that is too small floats and makes the
  room look smaller; get one big enough that the front legs of the seating sit on
  it.

Buy fewer, larger pieces. Many small decorations read as clutter; a few larger
textures read as intent.

## Move four: rearrange before you buy

Free and underrated. Pull the largest piece of furniture off the wall by even a
few centimetres — rooms breathe when furniture is not glued to the edges. Angle a
chair toward the seating instead of the TV. Group what belongs together. Live
with the new layout for a day before deciding it is wrong.

## Move five: one green thing

A single real plant adds life a room cannot fake. If you kill plants, pick a
tough one — a snake plant or pothos survives low light and irregular water. Put
it where you will see it and remember it. One plant, placed well, beats five you
forget to water.

## What to skip

- **New furniture** as a first move. Change the light and the wall first; you may
  find you do not need it.
- **Trend colors** you will tire of. On the wall you face daily, pick something
  you already know you like.
- **Buying storage to hide clutter** you could remove. Clearing beats concealing.

## The order that works

Clear the surfaces, then fix the light, then paint the one wall, then layer
texture, then rearrange, then add the plant. Each step makes the next look
better, so the room improves the whole weekend rather than only at the end.
Stand in the doorway when you finish — that is where you judge a room, and that
is where the change will show.
