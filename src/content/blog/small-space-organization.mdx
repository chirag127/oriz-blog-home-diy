Small homes do not need more storage. They need a system that decides where each
thing lives and makes putting it back the easy option. Most clutter is not a
shortage of space — it is a shortage of decisions. This guide gives you the
decisions.

Budget: mostly free. Time: a weekend, one zone at a time. Skill: none.

## The one rule that fixes most of it

Everything gets a home, and its home is close to where you use it. A pen lives
near where you write. Batteries live near the remote. When the home is obvious
and near, putting things away costs no effort, so they get put away. When the
home is far or vague, they pile up. That is the whole game.

## Step 1 — Sort before you store

Pick one zone: a single drawer, one shelf, the entryway. Empty it completely
onto a flat surface. Sort into four piles:

- **Keep here** — used in this zone, weekly or more.
- **Keep elsewhere** — belongs in another zone.
- **Fix or return** — broken, borrowed, or someone else's.
- **Out** — donate, recycle, bin.

Do not buy a single container until the "out" pile is gone. You cannot size
storage for stuff you are about to remove.

## Step 2 — Go up, not out

Floor space is scarce; wall and door space is not. Cheap wins that add capacity
without a bigger footprint:

- **Over-door racks** on the back of doors for shoes, cleaning spray, wrapping.
- **A single shelf** mounted 30 cm below the ceiling runs the whole perimeter of
  a room and holds everything you reach for twice a year.
- **Vertical dividers** stand baking trays, cutting boards, and lids on edge so
  you grab one without moving five.
- **Hooks** turn any bare wall into hanging storage for bags, tools, and mugs.

Measure the wall before you drill. Mark stud lines with a pencil, hang heavy
items on studs, and use wall anchors rated above the load for everything else.

## Step 3 — Contain by category, label the edge

Now — and only now — add containers. Group by how you *reach* for things, not by
what they are. A "phone chargers, cables, adapters" box beats three separate
tidy boxes you never open together.

Rules that keep containers working:

- **Clear or labeled.** If you cannot see in, write what is in it on the front
  edge where you look, not the top.
- **One layer deep where possible.** Buried is the same as lost.
- **Leave 20% empty.** A full container has nowhere to put the next thing, so
  the next thing lands on the counter.

## Step 4 — Build a drop zone

Every home needs one honest landing spot by the door: a tray for keys, a hook
for the bag, a small bowl for coins and receipts. This absorbs the daily churn
that would otherwise spread across the whole house. Ten minutes to set up, saves
ten minutes of hunting every morning.

## Step 5 — The five-minute reset

A system only survives if resetting it is quick. Once a day, walk one loop and
return anything out of place to its home. Because every item now has a near,
obvious home, the loop takes about five minutes. That daily loop is what keeps a
small space from sliding back into clutter.

## What to skip

- **Matching bin sets** bought before sorting. You will own the wrong sizes.
- **Deep bins on high shelves.** You will not dig; the bottom becomes a void.
- **"Organizing" that is really just rearranging.** If nothing left the house,
  you moved the problem, you did not solve it.

## A realistic first weekend

Saturday: entryway drop zone, plus the kitchen drawer that holds everything.
Sunday: one closet, top to bottom, using the four-pile sort and adding hooks or a
perimeter shelf. Two zones done, momentum built. Do one more zone each following
weekend and the whole home is sorted in a month, without a storage-store haul.
